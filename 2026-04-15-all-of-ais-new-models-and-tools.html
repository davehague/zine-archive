VI
Meta Super Intelligence Lab

Muse Spark:
Welcome Back to the Party

Meta's first new model release in over a year comes from their Super Intelligence Labs division, assembled under Alexander Wang through the $14 billion partial acquisition of Scale. Muse Spark is natively multimodal, ditching the Llama name entirely for a new architecture similar to Google's Gemini. The model supports tool use, visual chain of thought, and multi-agent orchestration.

Unlike competitors focusing on coding and enterprise, Muse Spark targets personal agents. Mark Zuckerberg wrote that it's particularly strong in “visual understanding, health, social content, shopping, games, and more.” It runs in three modes: instant, thinking, and contemplating (deep research).

Anthropic Platform Launch

Claude Managed Agents

16 million views on the announcement tweet. From prototype to launch in days.

Anthropic announced everything you need to build and deploy agents at scale. The platform pairs an agent harness tuned for performance with production infrastructure, including sandboxed environments, autonomous cloud execution, and toggleable permissions.

“There is a notable gap between what Anthropic's models are capable of and what businesses are using them for.”

Angela Jiang, Head of Product, Claude Platform

Common Agent Patterns:

Event-Triggered: A system flags a bug, the managed agent writes the patch and opens the PR. No human in the loop.

Scheduled: Daily briefs of X/Twitter activity, GitHub activity, what a team of agents is working on.

Fire & Forget: Humans trigger via Slack or Teams; long-horizon autonomous research tasks.

“You no longer need an engineer to run an overnight marketing analysis. You need one sharp operator and an afternoon. Set the schedule, set the guardrails, and walk away.”

Jared Orkin

The catch: “Someone still has to tune the prompt every Friday and act on the brief by 9am Monday. That's a job. The agent writes the brief, the operator runs the day.”

IX
Google Gemini

Notebooks:
Your Second Brain

It seems small at first, but Notebooks may be the biggest quality-of-life upgrade for Gemini users this year. Google has introduced personal knowledge bases shared across Google products, bringing the resource management power of NotebookLM directly into Gemini.

Users can organize documents, context, and custom instruction sets per project—effectively making every door into Google's AI lead to the same room. It consolidates the transportability of features across Google's sprawling surface areas.

“Most AI chatbots give you basic projects. Gemini just built you a second brain.”

Josh Woodward, Google

What Changed

Google's previous “Gems” feature was an unintuitive, partial version of projects. Notebooks replaces this with direct project management: collated resources, per-project custom instructions, and cross-product portability.

The Bigger Picture

Even if people like Google's models, the product suite is so spread across surface areas that it becomes overwhelming. Notebooks begins to consolidate—not the products themselves, but the features across them.

“This will be an even bigger improvement than if they had released Gemini 3.3.”
