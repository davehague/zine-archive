Before the Word "Podcast" Existed

In 2002, Carl Franklin started recording conversations and putting them on the web — calling them an "internet audio talk show for .NET developers." He had a mailing list of 25,000–30,000 people from his Visual Basic homepage and a training company called Franklin's .NET. When RSS feeds came around, he already had a hundred episodes and all the metadata in a database. When Apple made podcasting take off, it was just the next bump.

The Why Conversation

The Three Questions That Unlock Value Pricing — From Alan Weiss via Jonathan Stark

Why This?

Do you have to build this? Is there something you can get off the shelf and modify? By the time a prospect is talking to you, Carl notes, the answer is usually no — it's too complex, too much business logic to customize. So you write that down. You're documenting why a custom build is unavoidable, capturing the language they use, building the foundation for a proposal they'll recognize as their own words reflected back.

Why Now?

Can it wait a year? You're basically trying to talk them out of hiring you. Hopefully the answer is something like "the current application is hemorrhaging money." That's a ding ding ding. Carl observes they've usually waited too long by the time they're calling. The urgency isn't manufactured — it's real, and they want you to understand it. This is where the value lives.

Why Me?

You could hire a team from Bangalore for $30 an hour. Hopefully they'll say they did their research, found your reputation, read the testimonials. Carl's newest client found him through Reddit research. Another didn't realize until halfway through the project that he was Carl Franklin from .NET Rocks — "and like his whole attitude changed after that."

The Three‑Option Proposal

01

Option One: The MVP

A minimum viable product for less money. Doesn't include everything in the spec they wanted, but it covers the major things. The safety net. The "if budget is truly tight" option that still delivers real value.

02

Option Two: The Full Spec

Everything you all agreed on during phase one — the documentation and discovery. Every feature, every requirement, at a higher price. The complete vision, as scoped.

03

Option Three: The Works

Bells and whistles: penetration testing from a reputable security company, CSS design polish, accessibility, end-to-end testing, QA testers to bang on the product. Things typically not in a software spec. And they always pick option three.

The Mind Shift

Less Stress. More Money.

My customers love this. Because they know exactly how much they're going to pay, and I have guaranteed them that I'm going to keep working until they say it's done and until our spec is complete.

When asked about the financial impact of switching to value pricing, Carl doesn't hesitate: "Less stress for me, more money." There's no argument at the end of the project about bugs. No "I want to be paid to fix the bugs" / "We're not going to pay you to fix the bugs" blowup. The car lot metaphor says it all — imagine taking a car for a few months and then being told how much it costs. Nobody wants that. A fixed price, tied to value, gives everyone certainty. And when Carl told his new client "we're not going to stop working until you're satisfied," they replied: "Sounds too good to be true." Another guy chimed in: "Well, you haven't seen the number yet."

You Can't Fake Expertise

🎯

Know What to Ask

"It's easier for guys like us who've been around the block because we know what questions to ask and we know to recognize the answer as inappropriate or not right." The agents might give you a solution that works — but it's dumb. You wouldn't know that without experience.

🔍

The Framework Trap

Carl's telling example: your boss asks for a class that does X. You ask ChatGPT to write it. It works. But the framework already has that class built in. "The question should have been: is there anything in the tool set that I'm using that does this?"

⚡

Grunt Work, Not Thinking

Jonathan gets the most utility from AI "when I'm asking it to do some grunt work that I'm capable of doing. I just don't want to do it. Because then I can verify the results." If you don't know what correct looks like, you have no way to verify.

🛡️

Be Good First

"First of all, you have to not be a bullshitter. You can't BS your way into something like this. You actually have to do the work and you have to be good enough to do the work." Then show the world you can. Blog. Contribute to open source. Answer questions.
