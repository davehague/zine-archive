V
Spread V  ·  The Response

An anatomy of “No.”

Fourteen seconds into this conversation, the question lands: would you do this, Sam? Sam does not ask for details. He does not ask about the price or the couple or the venue.

He answers in four short sentences, and each one closes a door the previous one left open. It is less an opinion than a verdict, delivered at the pace of a man who has already picked what to watch on Netflix.

The pitch was a paid seat at a stranger’s wedding. The reply was a couch.

VII
Spread VIIThe Seat LedgerOpinion & Comedy

“If you invite me,
you’re an asshole.”

A comedian said it  ·  the host agreed

“I heard a comedian say this once,” the host recalls. The joke is short and hostile to the whole wedding industry: “If you invite me to your wedding, you’re an asshole.”

Then comes the part that matters for anyone building a ticketing site. “And I agreed with that.” It is a small confession, and it undercuts the market the conversation has just described.

“I can’t imagine that people want to go to weddings voluntarily.”

The word is “voluntarily.” An invitation is an obligation with a dress code, which is the comedian’s whole point. A ticket, by contrast, is a choice someone made on purpose, with their own money.

Whether anyone makes that choice is an open question, but 20,000 users on one site suggests the answer isn’t zero.
