II
The Problem · Why Vibe Checks Don't Scale

The Vibes Problem

You tweak a prompt. It feels better. There's no way to know, but overall things look good. Then the weeks go by.

Monday

Change something in the prompt, ship it. It overall feels better.

Tuesday

Ship it again. Still vibes to go off of.

A week later

The use case you shipped a fix for is broken.

Next week

The problem keeps getting worse. Nobody can prove whether something was fixed or is actually failing.

III
Definitions · What an Eval Isn't

Not a Unit Test.

The question SallyAnn gets most: is an eval just a unit test? Close cousins, but an agent breaks the assumptions a test relies on.

Traditional Software

The Unit Test

  • One input, one correct output
  • You can read all of the logic in the code
  • Pass or fail is a single comparison
AI Agent

The Eval

  • You can't read the logic. It comes out afterward, in the form of traces
  • Many valid outputs: a day-by-day itinerary, a budget breakdown
  • Multiple criteria, judged at once
Traces are the logs for AI. Evals are the tests.SallyAnn DeLucia

And the hard pill to swallow: you need traces and observability before you can do evals at all. A trace is every step the agent took to get from a user's message to an answer.

IV
The Field Guide · Types of Evals

Seven Ways to Judge

Today's workshop focuses on LLM-as-a-judge. But there's a whole toolbox around it.

No. 1

LLM as a Judge

A second LLM grades some aspect of your agent (QA, summarization, relevance) and returns a label plus an explanation.

No. 2

Decision Models

New, cheap and fast. Closer to a question and answer, returning a probability and a label. No explanation.

No. 3

Code Evals

For deterministic tests: is a word in the output, is it valid JSON. ROUGE and BLEU scores live here too.

No. 4

Annotations

Golden truths on golden datasets, sometimes synthetic. Human labels as a form of evaluation.

No. 5

Agent as a Judge

A full agent evaluates something in a different agent. Newer, seen over roughly the last six months. Suits complex success criteria.

No. 6 & 7

User Feedback & Business Metrics

The last three types are mostly for production use cases.

Sidebar

Build once, keep using it

The first four types work across your whole life cycle, in development and in production.

LLM judge, decision model, code, annotationsDev + Prod
Agent judge, feedback, business metricsProd-leaning
“You don't want to put a lot of investment in something you use in development and just throw it away.”

VI
Chapter One · The Method

Define What Good Means

The first step in any eval is writing down what good means. It's sometimes tricky, and the trick is to be concrete. Put yourself in the seat of the product person, the engineer, or any real stakeholder, and look at how users interact with the agent and how it responds.

For the travel agent, good in product terms is simple: it answers the follow-up questions the user asked and respects the trip's days and budget. Notice how much more useful that is than "helpful, accurate, good response."

Then keep it small. Write down a handful of qualities and build those first. Evals are an iterative thing, and the set grows as the application meets real usage. Treat writing criteria like describing success to a coworker.

Vague vs. Concrete

Helpful, accurate, good response

  • Follows the user's follow-ups
  • Matches the requested number of days
  • Keeps totals within the stated budget
Rule of Thumb

Judge one quality per eval

Research shows an LLM judge does well with one criterion at a time. Decision models and agent-as-a-judge can get away with more. Add evals over time instead.

Generic or Specific?

Some eval beats no eval

A specific eval for your use case is generally better. But templates like QA correctness, hallucination and session coherence are great for starting. Then make them more specific.

The Six-Part Recipe

6things to write down before you build your eval
1

The Task

What is the eval going off of? Define the thing you want judged.

2

What Success Looks Like

Describe it the way you would to a coworker or a stakeholder. Be specific and concrete.

3

What Failure Looks Like

The mirror image. What does a bad answer actually do?

4

What's Being Judged

What the eval is supposed to evaluate. One quality, not five.

5

Three Bullets: Pass

  • Bullet one
  • Bullet two
  • Bullet three
6

Three Bullets: Fail

  • Bullet one
  • Bullet two
  • Bullet three
Worked Example
Task

User sends a follow-up about their trip.

Success

Answers the follow-up directly, using the trip's details.

Failure

Falls back to a generic budget.

Why bullets and not a one-liner? These notes become the LLM prompt for your judge. The more concrete you are, the better the eval.

Live Build · Arize Playground

Build the Judge

Seven moves from traces to a saved evaluator, done live in the prompt playground.

01

Filter the spans

Pick what to judge. Root spans: parent ID = null. Tool calls: filter by name.

02

Save a dataset

Select the spans and add them to a new dataset.

03

Open the playground

Use the prompt playground for eval prompts. The agent playground tests agents end to end.

04

Write the template

Start blank. Center the LLM in its job, then paste your pass and fail bullets.

05

Map variables

Don't sweat variable names. Mapping connects the prompt to your exact data.

06

Choose labels

Correct / incorrect, or add partially correct, or not applicable. One label per run, plus an explanation.

07

Run, read, iterate

Run on your dataset, agree or disagree with the results, adjust the prompt. Then save the evaluator.

∞

Reuse everywhere

A saved evaluator is available across the platform, for the demo project or any app you bring in.

Pro tip

Alex, Arize's in-platform agent, can run the playground for you, look at the results, update the prompt and repeat. Best used after you've done your first pass by hand.

X
What's Next · Your First Week

Then keep going.

A working judge is the start of the loop. Here's what good teams do next.

  • A

    Alignment

    Annotate the examples in your dataset, run the eval, and check that it matches the human feedback.

  • O

    Online evals

    Run your evals continuously. Every trace you send in is evaluated and posted automatically, so you can monitor the app.

  • D

    Decision judges

    Convert an LLM judge into a decision-judge question and compare performance. See which is better.

  • S

    Signal

    A managed agent that reads your traces, finds issues, and can even make fixes.

  • E

    Every stakeholder

    Product, engineering, support, experts, security. Get them in the room to define success by their own outcomes.
