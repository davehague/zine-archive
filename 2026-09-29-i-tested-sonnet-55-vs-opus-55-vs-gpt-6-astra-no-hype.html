IV · Test 02 · Front-End Design

Checking In at Dune House

The brief: a landing page for a boutique hotel. There was little direction, so taste had to do the work.

Anthropic's models started with a handicap. Unlike GPT-6 Astra, they can't create their own images, so Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5 were told in their prompt that they had access to the Higgsfield API. All three reached for the same move: a giant hero image. “Anthropic lately loves these really big hero images,” he observes, and it isn't meant as a complaint.

Sonnet 5.5 built something that read like a real hotel site, with images front and center and a footer that echoed the hero. The font choice didn't land, and the check-availability pill was, bluntly, “really ugly.” Still, he called it pretty solid for a prompt that didn't give it much direction.

Opus 5.5 took creative liberties. The “Dune House” wordmark sits transparent and cut off. There's a small logo that reacts to the mouse, a glassmorphism availability panel and an orange-to-black palette. Numbers tick upward on scroll, a banner pauses on hover, the photos come cropped, and each room has its own date availability. His verdict on the booking widget next to Sonnet's: “This looks ugly as hell. This is much better.”

GPT-6 Astra had his favorite hero section of the three, and the cleanest page overall. He liked it more than Sonnet's, but it didn't destroy Sonnet. It was on par with Opus, and which one you prefer comes down to taste.

V · Test 03 · Let It Go Wild

Form over
Function

The prompt was a 3D travel dashboard that looks like something out of a sci-fi film, with plenty of leeway (and Higgsfield access for the Anthropic pair). Two models went for spectacle. One built something you could actually ship.

Sonnet 5.5

> status: off_the_rails

It went pretty crazy. I actually have no idea what I'm looking at. A zoomable globe of flights, generated city imagery, Paris rundowns, Tokyo-to-Bali fares, even a music bed.

Opus 5.5

> press [H] to flatten_globe

Fares from JFK, flight times, switchable hubs (LAX, LHR) and a key that “redraws the world around my departure city,” laying the globe flat. I don't even know what that means, but okay, that's kind of sick.

GPT-6 Astra

> mode: practical

Much more subdued. You pick departure, dates, number of travelers and cabin, then hit Explore for imagery and details. You could definitely turn this into something you would actually use.

VI · World of Tanks, Built From Scratch

Stalemate on the Browser Battlefield

Each model was asked to build its own version of World of Tanks in Three.js, running in a web browser. There were no asset packs. Everything was built from scratch. And the tester lost nearly every match.

750,000

Tokens · Sonnet 5.5The priciest single run of the shootout. Sonnet used the most tokens and took the longest to finish. Opus used 500K; Astra used about 300K.

Sonnet Fields a Tank Destroyer

Sonnet 5.5 · 750K tokens

A loading screen, a roster of tanks with stats for each and a TD7 tank destroyer to pick. Bots spawn, ammo types switch on hotkeys, the scope zooms, reload timers tick and repairs are required.

“I got destroyed,” he admits, “but this actually looked like it was working pretty well.”

Opus Delivers a Near-Twin

Opus 5.5 · 500K tokens

He picked the heavy tank this time. There were the same stat panels, with one minor UI overlap in the bottom-right corner. It had three round types, zoom, and a very similar map.

“Second time was not a charm, but it worked pretty much identically to Sonnet.”

Astra Calculates the Ricochet

GPT-6 Astra · ~300K tokens

There were four classes: light, medium, heavy and tank destroyer. It showed fewer stats and a simpler interface, but a better map and a UI he preferred. It also reads the angle of every hit and tells you when a shot ricochets.

A teammate stole the kill. “I'll take it.”

“So honestly, I would call it a tie.”Slight edge to Astra on tokens
