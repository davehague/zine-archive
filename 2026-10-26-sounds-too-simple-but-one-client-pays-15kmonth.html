II
02 — The Business

We become a company’s AI department.

Think of an IT department that handles everything IT — except Certified AI Partners does everything AI.

“Rather than locking things down, we unlock things and unlock their capacity to grow with AI.”

The spark was a podcast guest, Corey Ganon, describing an AI assessment for companies. “Like a light bulb just struck,” James says — he was working out in the gym at the time. A week later he called Brendan.

They began by walking into local Provo businesses — orthopedics, dental offices, insurance companies — offering a free 15-minute AI assessment. The problems were real. The budgets weren’t. Then came the pivot: “Why are we going for small fish? Why don’t we do this for bigger fish?”

V
05 — The Assessment

The $1,000 foot in the door.

The first client was a CEO who answered an advice request with: “I actually think my company needs this.”

The first big break came when they reached out to the CEO of a construction company covering Utah, Arizona and California. Again, they literally just asked for advice. He wrote back that he thought his company needed exactly this, and the first engagement began as a full paid assessment — priced at $1,000.

They asked where he saw bottlenecks, then asked for every person they should interview. A survey went out first, built around those bottlenecks and the software each person touched. At the end of the survey, each employee had to book a 30-minute call. With an AI note-taker running, the founders arrived at each call already knowing the answers to expound on.

Garbage in, garbage out

“There’s no special prompt. It’s more just making sure you get as much ammo before you go to Claude and spin up your presentation.”

All of it fed a clean, simple presentation for the C-suite: AI opportunities ranked, with the ROI of each based on what employees and executives had said about hours spent and what the team is paid. And they built mini working demos from redacted workpapers and invoices the staff sent over.

The sleepless-night question

“When was the last time you had a sleepless night because of something that happened in your business?” beats “What are you struggling with?” — which only earns lame answers.

The job: clearing the storm wells

The construction company maintains big storm wells, clearing debris and water when they clog. They receive an inspection report with an estimate of how long a clean will take, then build a priced proposal and send it to the HOA or property — who approve or deny it.

Based on everyone the founders interviewed, that took employees about an hour per proposal. The pair mapped how it works now, then mapped the steps AI could take off their plates, and what that would save.

“They gave us their problem, and then in front of them they’re seeing it being solved.”

The demo, built from the examples the client sent, counts the structures to be cleaned, estimates how much debris is in them, and prices the job accordingly. “Oh, you should have seen their faces,” says one founder. “They were like, ‘Oh my gosh, this is working.’”
