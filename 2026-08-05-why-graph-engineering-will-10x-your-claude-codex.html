The messy chat

One big question, one big answer. The writer is also the judge — like asking someone to write their own performance review, then being shocked when they call themselves a visionary.

The graph

A planner splits the question. Researchers work in parallel. A skeptic tries to kill the weak findings. A merger turns survivors into a one-page recommendation. Then you approve before acting.

Where the confusion comes from

Two things people mean by "graph."

A knowledge graph helps AI reason over relationships between things: this customer works at this company, this company uses this product, this issue relates to this feature. It shines where normal RAG struggles — when the answer requires connecting people across companies, topics, claims, and events.

Understand

How information connects.

An agent graph is about how work moves. A planner hands work to researchers, they run in parallel, a skeptic checks the findings, a synthesizer merges the parts, a human approves the final answer. This is the version you can start using today.

Where graph thinking earns its keep

The Real Workflow

Support

Not "answer the ticket."

The real workflow is understanding, researching, drafting, checking, and approving.

  1. Classify the issue
  2. Check account context
  3. Search docs & policy
  4. Draft the reply
  5. Checker reviews tone & risk
  6. Human approves refunds & angry customers
Content

Not "write a script."

Closer to how a real content lead actually works.

  1. Research → thesis → examples
  2. Hook, then draft script
  3. Checker: are examples specific? does the hook earn attention?
  4. Branch into titles, thumbnails, captions, B-roll
  5. "Does this sound like a human — or someone trapped in a SaaS onboarding flow?"
Coding

Not "write the code."

The model writing code is only one part. The leverage is everywhere else.

  1. Plan the change
  2. One agent edits
  3. Another reviews the diff
  4. Another runs tests
  5. Another checks the UI in a browser
  6. Human approves the pull request
One mistake to avoid

More agents don't
mean better output.

"Sometimes more agents mean five AI workers confidently repeating the same wrong idea."

The goal isn't the biggest graph. It's the smallest graph that improves the quality of work.

Remove the fake waiting — run independent jobs in parallel.
Separate workers from checkers.
Gate with a human where mistakes are expensive.
Stop when the answer is good enough — and leave behind the state.
Your first graph — this week

Draw it before
you automate it.

  1. 1Pick one weekly workflowResearching ideas, reviewing landing pages, analyzing customer feedback.
  2. 2Write the final output in one sentence"A one-page recommendation on whether this idea is worth testing."
  3. 3List the jobs a great human would doClarify, research customers, research competitors, find distribution, find risks, check, recommend.
  4. 4Draw arrows only where work truly dependsCustomer & competitor research run at once; the skeptic needs them first.
  5. 5Add one human gate before the expensive decisionLight for a private memo; strict for customer emails, deploys, or production data.
  6. 6Run it manually — onceJust the jobs and the arrows. No giant automation project required.
"Once you have one graph that works, you're not just prompting AI anymore — you're managing AI work."

The compounding payoff: the graph produces the work, but it also produces the memory that makes the next graph smarter. Context becomes the moat.
