III
The Foundation

A Project Is Just a Folder

The concepts sound intimidating — projects, skills, workspaces. But JJ strips it down: a project is literally a folder on your computer. A skill is literally a file that describes what you want to do. That’s it. You organize your Google Drive or your Notion by folders. Exact same concept, except this time Claude can go in and actually do some work alongside you.

JJ starts every project the same way: create a folder, open it in Co-work, and point Claude at it. Inside, the most important file is what he calls “my brain” — an MD file packed with working preferences, team members, communication style, and goals. Once Claude reads it, every conversation starts from a place of deep context instead of zero.

The unlock isn’t AI magic. It’s that you’re not starting over every time. You’re starting from a really good place where Co-work already knows about you, your preferences, all of that — and then it starts doing stuff with you as a partner.

“With co-work, you’re not starting over from the start.”

If you build up these projects, you’re starting from a really good place where co-work already knows about you, your preferences, all that kind of stuff, and then it just starts doing stuff with you as a partner.

— JJ Englert

VI
The Skill System

Build Once,
Invoke Forever

Skills are the secret weapon of the daily operating system. Think of a skill as a very detailed, reusable prompt stored as a file. Instead of telling Claude everything about your newsletter format each time, you build the skill once — covering subject lines, body segments, voice, evaluation criteria — and then just say “write the newsletter.” Claude invokes the skill and follows the instructions automatically. JJ has a skill for everything: Twitter, LinkedIn, YouTube, podcasting, newsletter writing. Each one captures his exact preferences so the output is consistent, high-quality, and distinctly his.
The email writing skill is a perfect example of the pattern. JJ tells Claude to analyze 46 sent emails from the last 30 days, extract his writing style, tone, and structure, then save it as a reusable voice profile. Now every email draft sounds like JJ wrote it personally. Claire describes the same approach for her newsletter: a slash command triggers a multi-step skill that reads the changelog, writes in her voice, formats for the platform, and pushes via API. She does a couple of edits and moves on with her life. The anti-to-do list concept captures the philosophy: identify things you never want to do again, then build skills to handle them.

The Anti-To-Do List

“Come up with a list of things you just never want to do again. I never want to have to first draft my emails again. I never want to show up to a meeting late because there’s a conflict on my calendar. Just burn down your anti-to-do list and build skills on all those.” — Claire Vo

JJ’s sub-advisory skill spins up three separate agents, each with a fresh context window and a defined persona. One represents the AI builder. Another is a security-conscious executive. A third is a concerned parent. Each reviews your work from their unique perspective and brings feedback back to you — before you ship it to the real audience. The Trust Spectrum

Progressive Trust:
Your Pace, Your Rules

Level 1: Draft Mode

AI drafts your emails but never sends. You review every word. The instruction is explicit: “Never send emails on my behalf. Only write them as drafts for me to review.”

Level 2: Read Access

You let AI read your inbox, calendar, and Slack. It ingests your patterns, builds your voice profile, and starts understanding your world — but still only acts with permission.

Level 3: Autonomous Agent

Scheduled tasks run before you wake up. Sub-agents review your work independently. Your morning debrief is ready at 7:30 AM with a full plan of action, drafted replies, and meeting prep.

Level 4: Shadow You

“A shadow JJ that actually just operates completely independent of you and you’re in the Bahamas hanging out and agent JJ is actually doing your job for you.” The far edge of the spectrum.

“It’s a trade of information for productivity. You give the AI a little bit of information, it can help you be very, very productive. You’ve got to figure out where you feel comfortable on that spectrum.”
— Claire Vo
