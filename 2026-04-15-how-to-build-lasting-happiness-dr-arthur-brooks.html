The Macronutrients of Happiness

Feelings of happiness are evidence of the actual phenomenon — like the smell of the turkey, not the turkey itself. To get happier, Brooks argues, you need to understand its component parts just like you understand protein, carbohydrates, and fat. There are three macronutrients of human happiness, and you need them in balance and abundance.

Enjoyment

A prefrontal cortex phenomenon. Unlike pleasure, which happens automatically in the limbic system, enjoyment is managed, shared, and made permanent through people and memory. "If you're doing it alone, you're probably doing it wrong."

Satisfaction

Achieving something with struggle. Only humans want to struggle. Satisfaction equals what you have divided by what you want. "Don't always work the numerator — manage your wants, because when your wants go down, your satisfaction rises and stays high."

Meaning

The answer to three questions: Why do things happen? Why am I doing what I'm doing? Why does my life matter and to whom? The crisis of our time — the #1 predictor of depression and anxiety is saying "my life feels meaningless."

The Dark Matter Between Our Ears

Reptilian Brain

Brainstem + cerebellum. Gathers data below your level of awareness: lights, air temperature, ambient signals. Shared with snakes and lizards.

~40 million years old

Limbic System

Translates raw data into emotions — signals of threat or opportunity. Negative emotions (fear, anger, disgust, sadness) and positive emotions are not "bad" or "good." There is literally more tissue devoted to negative emotions than to positive.

2–40 million years old

Prefrontal Cortex

The C-suite. 30% of your brain by weight. Takes emotional signals and — if you're mature — lets you decide how to act. This is where enjoyment lives. Pleasure is limbic; enjoyment is prefrontal.

~2 million years old

The Four Idols

After Thomas Aquinas · Summa Theologiae · 1265

◆

Money

Resources and wealth. People from real poverty develop this idol from fear of going back. People from real wealth develop it because that's how they understand themselves. The least likely: those indistinctly in the middle.

★

Power

Influence over other people. Not inherently evil — but when it becomes an intrinsic motivation, it changes how you relate to the people you care about most. It always leads to regret.

♥

Pleasure

Not just feeling good — also comfort, security. Checking your stock portfolio every day is the same circuit as the comfy covers instead of the gym. "A lot of people are like, 'I don't care about pleasure. Let's see how rich I am today.' It's the same thing."

▲

Fame

Honor, admiration, prestige. The desire to be liked and accepted by the right people. The admiration of strangers has neurochemical benefits rooted in our Pleistocene brains. "Makes you insane."

"Everybody is most beguiled by one. And when they know what their weakness is, they have pure strength."

The Hedonic Treadmill

The Striver's Curse

Satisfaction = Haves ÷ Wants

Most Olympic athletes who win a medal experience clinical depression in the wake of their victory. We're wired for progress, not arrival. When the goalpost keeps moving, you get diminishing satisfaction. The antidote isn't having more — it's wanting less. Brooks practices a "reverse bucket list": naming attachments and ambitions, then crossing them out. "Easy come, easy go. I'm going to be better than fine. I'm free."

The Meaning of Your Life

The meaning of life is the answer to three questions. Big problems are just a bunch of little problems. If AI can answer your question, it's not a meaning question. Ask it "Why am I alive? For what would I die?" and it gives you nothing — because you can't answer it. You can only live with it.

Coherence

Why do things happen the way they do?

When people fall into conspiracy theories, it's a cry for coherence — they don't have a sense of meaning. Don't throw data at them. Provide a better way to find coherence as an act of love.

Purpose

Why am I doing what I'm doing?

We're made for progress, not to arrive at a goal. 30% of people on strong diets who hit their goal weight develop an eating disorder because they want the scale to keep going down. Direction matters more than destination.

Significance

Why does my life matter — and to whom?

The love problem. You need an explanation for the universe, goals and direction, and love. Those things together are the macros of meaning.

Brooks' Morning Protocol

4:30 AM

Rise in the Brahma Muhurta

An hour and 36 minutes before dawn — "the creator's time" in Sanskrit. Research shows being conscious while the sun rises improves productivity, creativity, focus, and happiness.

4:45–5:45

Physical Fitness

Seven days a week. Zone 2 cardio, resistance training, structured to protect joints for decades ahead. Manages high negative affect from the first moment of the day.

6:30–7:00

Metaphysical Fitness

Daily mass, combined with Vipassana meditation and Stoic philosophy. "Everybody's got to find their thing — a form of transcendence, focused on things greater than yourself every single day."

~7:00

Caffeine & Fuel

350mg caffeine as a bolus, 2.5 hours after waking to clear adenosine. Then 60g protein in first meal — Greek yogurt, rich in tryptophan. Creatine monohydrate (10g) in electrolytes pre-workout.

~8:00

3.5 Hours of Creative Work

"I can't write for 3.5 hours unless I set my day up in a particular way and I'm not unbearable to live with because my negative affect has been managed."

Happiness Is Love,
Full Stop.

01

Eye Contact

Women get three times as much oxytocin from eye contact. When men are cooling in a marriage, they look at you less. "The marriage microwave."

02

Always Be Touching

More important for men than they understand. Casual, non-sexual touch produces vasopressin — defense and strength. When women are cooling, they touch less. ABT.

03

Have Fun Together

Negative emotionality can be drowned in shared positive emotionality. Go ride bikes, walk the beach, watch a movie. Joy covers a multitude of sins.

04

Pray Together

Joint metacognition fuses right hemispheres. Reading poetry to each other is "almost narcotic for people who love each other." More intimate than anything else.

"We bond deeply to somebody who completes us, not who copies us. You want somebody who really makes you bigger and better. That's not dating yourself — that's narcissism, not hotness."
